# Home monitoring technology partners

Research date: **14 October 2026**. Prepared for Proactive Wellness, Durban, South Africa.

## Recommendation

Build the first service around a reliable connected blood-pressure cuff, a glucose meter for members who need one, and a weight scale or fingertip oxygen monitor when prescribed. Invest in onboarding, dependable transmission and a clear nurse review workflow before adding continuous wearables. Technology should help a clinician act on useful information; a device transmitting continuously does not itself provide continuous clinical care.

The first commercial conversations should be with **OMRON/HiTech Therapy, Roche Diabetes Care South Africa, SSEM Mthembu Medical and Vodacom Business**. Evaluate **Medi Response** for an optional, separately contracted fall/SOS service. Keep **Abbott CGM, TytoCare and Quro Medical** as targeted pathways. Withings is a strong technical benchmark, but its published cloud locations conflict with the project's present South African data-residency requirement.

These are candidates for evaluation, not confirmed partnerships. No suppliers have been contacted, prices negotiated, products ordered or regulatory approvals independently verified. Evidence below comes from manufacturers, service providers, regulators and a device-validation registry. A local distributor listing demonstrates a route to market, not stock of a particular connected model or permission to integrate its data.

## Fit with the membership model

**Connect — R995/month:** education, reminders, care coordination and member-entered readings can remain the starting point. Members who already own suitable devices should be able to share results without being forced into a particular ecosystem, subject to device provenance and validation checks.

**Monitor — R1,700/month:** model the economics of a loan BP cuff first. Add a glucose meter, scale or oxygen monitor according to the member's care plan. The monthly cost model needs hardware depreciation, consumables, connectivity, onboarding, nurse review time, failed-transmission follow-up, replacements, cleaning and returns. These are proposed operating choices, not changes to the agreed prices or benefits.

**Concierge — R2,650/month:** a nurse-carried examination kit or an independently priced safety service may add value, but quoted costs and staffing should decide what can be included. Continuous ECG and hospital-at-home need their own clinical service agreement rather than a passive stream of alerts into a weekday queue.

**CGM economics:** two Libre 2 sensors at Abbott's listed recommended price cost **R2,178 and cover 28 days**. For a simple 26-sensor annual budget, that is **R28,314/year**, or **R2,359.50/month averaged over 12 months**, before clinical review and other costs. This calculation explains why blanket CGM inclusion would exceed the entire Monitor fee. Medical-aid eligibility or negotiated sponsorship should be established member by member; coverage is not guaranteed. [Abbott price and funding information](https://www.freestyle.abbott/en-za/cost-and-coverage.html).

## Proposed first pilot

This is a suggested commercial and workflow pilot, not a prescribed clinical protocol. The clinical lead should set eligibility, measurement schedules, thresholds and escalation rules.

1. **Bench evaluation:** compare two BP models and one glucose integration with fictional/test data. Include a scale and oxygen monitor only where a supported interface is available. Test timestamps, units, device/member assignment, missing readings, duplicate uploads and offline recovery.
2. **Small service pilot:** consider 20–30 consenting members for 8–12 weeks after clinical and data arrangements are approved. Include some participants without a suitable smartphone. Start with stable chronic-care needs; use a separate service for acute or continuous-response needs.
3. **Measure operational value:** successful scheduled transmissions, time to first usable reading, time spent supporting each member, review/response time during staffed hours, false flags, device returns and total cost per active member. Agree pass criteria before enrolment. A small pilot cannot establish reductions in hospital admissions on its own.
4. **Add advanced pathways selectively:** CGM for clinician-selected diabetes cases with funding; a nurse tele-exam kit; then ECG/continuous monitoring through a partner with the required review service.

## Questions to send before choosing a partner

- **Exact product and SA supply:** Which SKU, firmware, intended use and accessories are offered here? Who is the authorised local supplier, and what SAHPRA establishment licence and applicable product status can they document? What can be verified against [SAHPRA's medical-device resources](https://www.sahpra.org.za/medical-devices/)? Do not treat a CE mark or US clearance as proof of SA supply authorisation.
- **Clinical evidence:** Is the precise BP device/cuff combination on an appropriate validation list such as [STRIDE BP](https://www.stridebp.org/bp-monitors/)? What evidence supports the intended populations, and what measurements are directly measured versus estimated? For pulse oximetry, ask for evidence across skin pigmentation and low-perfusion conditions; [FDA's overview](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters) explains why limitations matter.
- **Integration rights:** Can we receive readings in our own app through a supported, licensed SDK/API? Obtain sample data, a sandbox, event/webhook details, offline behaviour, timestamps/time zones, device identity, quality flags, versioning and support commitments. A consumer PDF report is not an automated clinical integration.
- **South African data residency:** Can all identifiable data, raw sensor data, backups, support logs and subprocessors remain in the approved SA environment? Where does data travel before we receive it? Who can access it from overseas? Require a documented data-flow diagram and contractual answer. A locally hosted copy does not resolve an overseas vendor-cloud dependency.
- **Consent and exit:** How are member authorisation, family permissions and revocation handled? Can complete data be exported in a documented format, deleted where appropriate, and migrated when the contract ends? Who owns derived data, and may it be used for vendor model training?
- **Loan-fleet operation:** Can devices be securely erased and reassigned? What are the cleaning rules, consumable replacement, serial-number tracking, calibration/verification, battery life, repair times and warranty terms? What continues to work during power or network loss?
- **Clinical responsibility:** Who monitors which signals, at what hours, with what acknowledgement target? Who responds when no reading arrives or no clinician acknowledges a flag? Which service takes responsibility after hours? What is the handover and response agreement for emergencies?
- **Full cost:** Request quotes at 25, 100 and 500 active members, stating VAT, hardware, consumables, platform/API minimums, SIM/data, onboarding, training, warranty, replacements, cancellation and export charges. Separate quoted prices from retail benchmarks.

## Technology choices to keep out of the first release

Do not replace validated cuffs or glucose devices with unvalidated camera, watch or ring estimates. In particular, the FDA warns against watches or rings that claim to measure glucose on their own without piercing the skin; that warning is distinct from displaying readings from a real CGM. [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/do-not-use-smartwatches-or-smart-rings-measure-blood-glucose-levels-fda-safety-communication).

Avoid building several vendor-cloud integrations before one complete device-to-clinician workflow is proven. Keep the app's device layer adaptable, retain source/device provenance with each reading, and make missing data visible. A future integration aggregator could reduce engineering work, but its residency, vendor permissions and fees need the same checks as a direct partner.

The current app remains a software prototype with fictional data. This research does not add device connectivity, establish a clinical partnership, or certify a device or care service.
